## First principles

1. What did we learn? A motion system that measures before it moves can only move what it can measure. A key makes a node identifiable, not animatable.
2. Foundations: FLIP needs a box for the first and last frame. animate.ts reads boxes from geometry attributes (x, y, width, height, r, x1..y2) or a text's x and y; a `g` has none of its own, so it falls through to a swap.
3. More: any keyed control drawn as a group (legend chips, list headers, future buttons) will snap the same way. Splitting the visual text from the hit rect is the reusable pattern.
4. Limit: the split puts focus and hit target on two nodes, so the hover underline needs `:has()` and a host that queries `[data-maya=more]` now gets two nodes.
